# The pitch, creases and ground anatomy

The pitch is the 22-yard (20.12 m) clay strip at the center of the oval. At
each end stand three stumps topped by two bails — together, the wicket. The
POPPING CREASE, 4 feet in front of the stumps, is the batter's safe ground —
running batters must ground bat or body behind it to avoid run outs, and a
bowler's front foot must land at least partly behind it or the delivery is a
no-ball. The BOWLING CREASE runs through the stumps; RETURN CREASES box the
bowler's landing zone sideways. Around the pitch, the 30-yard circle marks
powerplay fielding restrictions; the outer rope is the boundary. Pitch
character decides matches: hard, dry surfaces favor batting and spin as they
wear; grassy or fresh surfaces help pace and swing; venues develop
reputations — Chennai's Chepauk traditionally grips and turns, Bengaluru's
Chinnaswamy (small boundaries, altitude) is a six-hitting ground, Wankhede's
red soil gives pace and bounce under lights, Ahmedabad's Narendra Modi Stadium
(the world's largest, hosting recent finals) offers big square boundaries.
Dew is a major IPL factor: evening moisture makes gripping the ball hard for
the side bowling second, which is one reason captains often chase after
winning the toss in night games. This database's venue tool reports matches
hosted and average innings scores per ground — a data view of these
characteristics.
