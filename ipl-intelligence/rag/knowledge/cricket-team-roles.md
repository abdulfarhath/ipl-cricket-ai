# Team roles: players, captain, coaches and support staff explained

Playing roles: OPENERS face the new ball and exploit powerplay fielding
restrictions; TOP ORDER (Nos. 3-4) builds the innings; MIDDLE ORDER
accelerates; a FINISHER (Nos. 5-7) specializes in death-overs hitting under
pressure. The WICKETKEEPER-BATTER keeps through all 20 overs and often opens
or finishes. ALL-ROUNDERS contribute with both bat and ball, giving the
captain a sixth bowling option. Bowling roles: new-ball SWING bowlers strike
in the powerplay; PACERS with yorkers and slower balls own the death overs;
SPINNERS (leg-spin, off-spin, left-arm orthodox, mystery spin) squeeze the
middle overs. The CAPTAIN sets fields, chooses bowling changes, decides
reviews and calls the toss — tactical decisions ball by ball.

Support staff (the roles franchises maintain): the HEAD COACH sets strategy,
selection direction and team culture; a BATTING COACH works on technique,
match-ups and range hitting; a BOWLING COACH designs plans (yorker execution,
wide-line fields, matchup bowling); a FIELDING COACH drills catching,
boundary-riding and throwing; the PHYSIOTHERAPIST manages injuries and
recovery; STRENGTH AND CONDITIONING coaches manage workloads; the TEAM ANALYST
prepares data — matchups (batter vs bowler type), phase plans and opposition
tendencies — the professional analog of what this assistant's database tools
compute; a TEAM MANAGER handles logistics, and franchises add mentors and
directors of cricket above the coaching group. Note on data: this assistant's
match database (from ball-by-ball records) does not include staff rosters;
names of a team's coaches can be loaded through its manual CSV importer, and
the roles themselves are explained here.
