# Match officials, their roles and umpire signals

Every IPL match has two on-field umpires (one at the bowler's end ruling on
LBW, no-balls and caught-behinds; one at square leg watching run outs,
stumpings and waist-high full tosses; they swap ends each over), a third (TV)
umpire who rules on referrals with replays — line calls, boundary checks,
clean catches, DRS reviews, and since 2020 the front-foot no-ball check on
every delivery — plus a fourth/reserve umpire managing balls and logistics,
and a match referee who oversees the code of conduct, hears disciplinary
matters and imposes fines (for example over-rate penalties on captains).
Standard signals: index finger raised — OUT. Both arms crossed and uncrossed
above the head — six. Arm swept horizontally to the side repeatedly — four.
Both arms stretched horizontally — wide (one extra run, ball re-bowled). One
arm extended horizontally — no-ball (one run, free hit next ball, signalled by
circling a raised arm). Arm raised vertically — bye (runs without bat contact,
not against the bowler); knee touched with raised leg — leg bye (off the body).
Arms crossed low with palms down — dead ball. TV-referral: mimicking a TV
rectangle with both hands. DRS flow: a side signals "T" within seconds of the
decision; the TV umpire checks ball-tracking and UltraEdge; "umpire's call"
outcomes keep the on-field decision and the review is retained. From 2024 the
IPL's Smart Replay System put ball-tracking and split-screen replays directly
with the TV umpire for faster, more consistent decisions. This database
records every match's umpires, TV umpires and referees — ask for any match's
officials or an umpire's career match count.
