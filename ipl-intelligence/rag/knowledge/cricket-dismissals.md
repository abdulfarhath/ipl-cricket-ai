# The ways a batter gets out (dismissals / wickets)

Common dismissals: BOWLED — the delivery hits the stumps and dislodges a bail.
CAUGHT — a fielder (or keeper or bowler, "caught and bowled") catches the ball
on the full after it touches the bat or glove. LBW (leg before wicket) — the
ball would have hit the stumps but strikes the batter's body first, subject to
pitching and impact rules (a ball pitching outside leg stump can never be LBW).
RUN OUT — a fielder breaks the stumps while the batters are short of the crease
attempting a run; NOT credited to the bowler; either batter can be run out,
including the non-striker. STUMPED — the keeper breaks the stumps while the
batter is out of the crease playing (not attempting a run); credited to the
bowler and only possible standing up, usually to spin. HIT WICKET — the batter
breaks their own stumps with bat or body while playing; credited to the bowler.
Rare modes: obstructing the field, hit the ball twice, timed out, and retired
out (a batter who retires without injury and does not resume). Retired hurt is
NOT a dismissal — the batter may return. Bowler-credited wickets (counted in
bowling figures and Purple Cap): bowled, caught, caught and bowled, LBW,
stumped, hit wicket. Run outs and the rare modes are team wickets only — this
database follows exactly that convention. A "wicket maiden" is a maiden over
containing a wicket. When ten of the eleven batters are out, the side is "all
out" and the innings ends (the last batter cannot bat alone).
