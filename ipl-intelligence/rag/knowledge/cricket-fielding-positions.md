# Cricket fielding positions and ground map

The field is split by an imaginary line through both sets of stumps: the OFF
side (the side the batter's bat faces) and the LEG or ON side (behind the
batter's legs); positions flip for left-handers. Close catching positions:
wicketkeeper (behind the stumps), slips (beside the keeper, numbered first,
second, third slip), gully (squarer of the slips), leg slip and leg gully
(mirror images on the leg side), silly point and short leg (very close, in
front of the bat). Ring positions on the off side, moving from behind square
to straight: third man (behind the keeper on the off side), backward point,
point (square of the wicket), cover, extra cover, and MID-OFF (straight, near
the bowler on the off side). On the leg side, from straight to fine: MID-ON
(straight, near the bowler on the leg side), midwicket (between mid-on and
square leg), square leg (level with the batter's crease, where the square-leg
umpire stands), backward square leg, and fine leg (behind square, near the
keeper). Any ring position pushed to the boundary takes the prefix "deep" or
"long": long-off and long-on are the straight boundary riders behind the
bowler; deep cover, deep point, deep midwicket, deep square leg, deep fine leg
and deep third man patrol the rope. "Short" means closer than the ring (short
midwicket), "silly" means dangerously close. Captains set fields within the
rules: during the T20 powerplay only two fielders may be outside the 30-yard
circle; afterwards up to five; never more than two leg-side fielders behind
square (a no-ball if breached); typical death-overs fields stack boundary
riders at long-off, long-on, deep midwicket and deep cover for yorker plans.
