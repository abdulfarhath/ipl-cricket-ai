# IPL playing conditions and in-match rules

Each innings lasts a maximum of 20 overs. The powerplay covers overs 1-6, when
only two fielders may stand outside the 30-yard circle; from overs 7-20 up to
five (four before 2015-style tweaks in wider T20 rules) fielders may be outside
the circle, and leg-side fielders behind square are limited to two at all
times. A bowler may bowl at most 4 overs. Each side may field at most four
overseas players in its XI. A no-ball for overstepping gives the batting side
one run plus a free hit on the next delivery, on which the batter cannot be out
bowled, caught, lbw or stumped (run out remains possible). If scores finish
level, the match is decided by a Super Over: each side bats one over with two
wickets in hand; if the Super Over also ties, further Super Overs follow.
Since 2023, the Impact Player rule lets each team name five substitutes and
bring one into the game at an innings break or fall of wicket/over break; the
Impact Player can bat and bowl fully, but using one means only three overseas
players may be in the starting XI if the Impact Player is overseas — effectively
teams play 12 with one restriction. Strategic timeouts pause play twice per
innings (one chosen by each side within set over windows). The IPL uses DRS
(Decision Review System): each side has two unsuccessful review chances per
innings, covering dismissals and, since recent seasons, wides and no-balls for
height and off-side. Slow over rates draw fines and, in recent playing
conditions, on-field penalties such as an extra fielder inside the circle for
overs bowled beyond the scheduled time.
