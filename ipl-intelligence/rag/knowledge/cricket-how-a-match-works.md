# How an IPL/T20 cricket match works, start to finish

Two teams of eleven play one innings each. Before play, captains exchange team
sheets and the coin toss decides which side bats or bowls first (in the IPL,
captains may finalize their XI after the toss). The batting side sends two
batters to the crease; the bowling side spreads eleven fielders including a
wicketkeeper. Play proceeds in overs — six legal deliveries bowled from one end
by one bowler; each new over is bowled from the opposite end by a different
bowler (no bowler may bowl consecutive overs, maximum four overs each in T20).
The two batters score runs by running between the wickets, or by hitting
boundaries: four runs if the ball reaches the rope after touching the ground,
six if it clears it on the full. The innings lasts 20 overs or until ten
wickets fall, whichever comes first. Overs 1-6 are the powerplay (fielding
restrictions); a strategic timeout break occurs in set windows. At the halfway
point the sides swap: the team batting second must exceed the first team's
total ("the chase") within its own 20 overs. Each dismissal brings the next
batter in; two batters always bat together — their combined scoring stand is a
partnership, measured in runs and balls, ending when one is dismissed. The
match ends when the chase succeeds (won by N wickets remaining), the chasing
side is bowled out or runs out of overs short (won by N runs), or scores tie —
then a one-over-per-side Super Over decides it. The winning side earns 2
league points. A Player of the Match is named. Rain-shortened games use the
DLS method to reset targets; a washout gives each side 1 point.
